## What this says

1. **No layout parser knows what a question is.** Docling, MinerU and Marker have no QUESTION, OPTION, SIDEBAR, OBJECTIVE, ANSWER or SPEECH_BUBBLE concept; they label everything "text"/"list_item". Their BODY precision of ~0.5 is not sloppiness — it is the gold's questions, options, sidebars and objectives all arriving as BODY. **Roles are a layer the ecosystem must build itself, deterministically, on top of the parser's geometry.**
2. **The current XY-cut is the only candidate with a question labeller, and it is not safe:** precision 0.69 means 3 of 10 "questions" it emits are headings, instructions, objectives or table headers (11 heading-as-question + 23 non-question-as-question events on 38 pages — 02 §3). Its sidebar detector (0.45 / 0.43) is a coin flip.
3. **Marker is the only candidate that recognises tables and formulas as objects** (1.00 / 1.00 on the gold pages it ran); Docling finds half of the tables; nobody returns formulas as anything but flattened text except Marker.
4. **Caption association** is solved well enough by Marker (0.95 / 1.00) and Docling (0.90 / 0.79) to build the `caption_of` relation of the SDM; the current pipeline's caption regex (0.85 / 0.74) is competitive because captions are lexical ("Hình 3.1").
5. The VLM, prompted for roles, produced roles on 2 % of blocks (it ignored the format on 20 of 22 pages). A 3B model is not a role labeller; a larger one might be, at a cost that does not fit a single Mac.

## Consequence for the architecture (feeds 11 and 19)

The role stage must be **separate and auditable**: geometry (box colour, indentation, column, position relative to figures) + typography (bold/size where available) + lexicon (stage labels KHỞI ĐỘNG/LUYỆN TẬP, directive verbs, "M:" model answers, "?" answer slots, "Em có biết") + document conventions per publisher series. Its output must carry its own confidence and be gated like text: a QUESTION whose verifier says HEADING is withheld (10, `role_conflict`). Until such a stage exists and is measured ≥ 0.95 precision on questions, **no automatically labelled question should be shown to a child as a graded prompt** — which is consistent with the WAL-204 finding that the neighbour-unit question selection was the third failure.
